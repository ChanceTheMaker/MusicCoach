Your quick-start primer

Piano: keys, fingers and chords

C is immediately left of a group of two black keys. Finger numbers run from thumb (1) to little finger (5), on either hand. A major triad uses root, major third and fifth; a minor triad lowers the third by a semitone. An inversion changes which chord tone is lowest. An arpeggio plays chord tones one at a time.

Drums: sticking, rudiments and coordination

R and L mean right and left hand. Singles alternate R L; doubles use R R L L. A paradiddle combines singles and doubles: R L R R L R L L. Here, snare and rim pads distinguish the two hands; the software grades the pad, not your actual hand. Simultaneous highlighted pads are a coordinated hit. These pad studies teach timing and patterns; rebound, grip and acoustic drum technique need practice on an appropriate surface.

Rhythm: pulse, subdivision and practice modes

The tempo is beats per minute. Two eighth notes or four sixteenth notes fill one quarter-note beat. Learn waits for the correct note or pad; Rhythm keeps moving and scores timing; Demo plays the pattern without awarding progress. Begin slowly, repeat a clean phrase three times, then raise the tempo a little.

Sequencer: build a complete groove

A 16-step pattern divides a four-beat bar into sixteenth notes. Choose a lane, then toggle its steps on the pads. Outlines show the target; filled pads show your pattern. Add kick, snare, hats and clap. Play combines the drum pattern with a melodic part. Demo lets you hear the target first.
